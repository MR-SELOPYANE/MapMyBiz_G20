📘 Module 11: Business Analytics & Decision Making

Learn how to use your business data to make smart, confident decisions

🎯 Learning Objectives

In this module, you will understand what business analytics means for small business owners and how it can help you grow. You’ll learn how to collect useful data from your sales, customers, and costs in a simple and practical way. You’ll discover how to turn numbers into real insights — like which products are selling well and which aren’t. You’ll also learn how to use this information to improve your pricing, manage inventory, and plan better marketing. Most importantly, you’ll be able to make decisions based on facts instead of guesses, giving you more control over your business success.

📊 1. What Is Business Analytics?

Business analytics means using real information from your business to make better choices. You don’t need expensive tools or complicated software — just a few simple records you can keep on paper or in a notebook.

Ask yourself basic but powerful questions: How many products did you sell this week? Which item brought in the most profit? What are customers saying about your service? The answers help you see what’s really happening in your business.

For example, imagine you sell five different products. Analytics might show that muffins make up 60% of your total profit. That tells you clearly: you should focus more on baking and selling muffins.

📈 2. Track the Right Data (Simple & Practical)

You don’t need to track everything — just the key details that help you grow. Focus on collecting a few important pieces of information each week.

Start by recording your sales by product — how many of each item you sold. Then track your total revenue, which is the total money you earned. Next, write down your costs, including stock, transport, and packaging. From this, you can calculate your profit by subtracting your costs from your revenue. Finally, note any customer feedback — what people liked or what they complained about.

💡 Start small: Just write down three things every Friday — your top-selling item, your biggest cost, and one comment from a customer. Over time, this builds a powerful picture of your business.

🔍 3. Ask the Right Questions

Data only becomes useful when you ask the right questions. Numbers on their own don’t help — but when you use them to find answers, they become powerful tools.

Ask: What product is selling the best? If one item is popular, consider making more of it. What isn’t selling? If something sits on the shelf, stop spending money on it. When are you busiest? Use this to plan your schedule or run special promotions. Which customers keep coming back? Try to find more people like them. Are your costs going up? Look for cheaper suppliers or ways to reduce waste.

The golden rule is simple: don’t guess. Always check the numbers before making a decision.

📉 4. Use Simple Reports to See Trends

You don’t need fancy graphs or dashboards — a simple table is enough to spot trends. For example, a weekly sales tracker can show you how your business is changing over time.

Weekly Sales Tracker:

Week Muffins Sold Vetkoek Sold Total Profit
Week 1 30 50 R850
Week 2 45 40 R920
Week 3 60 35 R1,100

This table shows that muffin sales are rising while vetkoek sales are dropping. The insight? Muffins are becoming more popular. You could try a “Buy 2, Get 1 Free” offer to grow that trend even faster.

✅ 5. Make Better Decisions with Data

Data helps you solve real business problems with confidence. Let’s look at two examples.

If you feel busy but aren’t making much money, check your numbers. You might find that vetkoek sells a lot but only makes R2 profit per piece, while muffins sell less but earn R8 each. The smart decision? Focus on selling more muffins and consider slightly increasing the price of vetkoek.

If you don’t know who your customers are, collect small observations over time. You might discover that 80% of your muffin buyers are office workers who stop by between 7 and 9 AM. With this knowledge, you can arrive earlier on weekdays and bring more muffins to meet the demand.

Every decision backed by data is a step toward a stronger, smarter business.

🔄 6. Review & Improve Weekly

Set up a simple weekly routine to keep learning and improving. Every Friday, take 15 minutes to review your sales and expenses. Ask yourself: What worked well this week? What didn’t go as planned?

Based on what you learn, make one small change for next week. Maybe stock more of your top seller, adjust your prices, or change your location. Then repeat the process.

“Success isn’t about being perfect. It’s about learning one thing each week and getting a little better.”
